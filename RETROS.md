## Sprint 3 Retro — 2026-07-23 (Jul 20–23; goal MET, 5/5 committed issues closed — #26/#24/#28/#29/#30, plus umbrella #9; #30 closed with its human tier-agreement AC explicitly deferred to Sprint 4; 4 bugs filed to Backlog, not fixed — #34/#35/#36/#37)

What worked:
- The two-surface split held under pressure, unprompted. Claude Code hit the null-domain bug mid-verification and stopped to surface it as an architecture question rather than fixing under momentum — exactly what CLAUDE.md instructs, on the one day when fixing it would have felt faster. It also self-corrected its own "board auto-add failed 3 for 3" framing to "1 of 3, cause unconfirmed" during the investigation, filing both findings honestly rather than forcing one explanation.
- Verification produced findings to the last hour — and the last one came from the demo dry run. #29's forced malformed-response test, then #30's three batches surfacing the competitor over-trigger, the disqualifier drift, fabricated headcounts, and a latent ICP ambiguity. The competitor bug alone would have shipped straight into Sprint 4's hot alerts; a scorer that zeroes good leads makes the alert layer worthless.
- Cost became measured, not aspirational. 33 scored leads: median $0.0095, max $0.0150, 100% under target — and the max is bounded by design (#20's 4,000-char cap, #22's 5-item news cap), not by luck. #29's $0.007 headline turned out to be a sparse-lead outlier; correcting our own claim downward is worth more than the original number.

What didn't:
- Three separate bugs, one root cause: the config says what to score, never where the boundaries are or how to weigh evidence. scoring_dimensions carry key, weight, ideal, and notes; hard_disqualifiers are four bare English phrases with no definition and no validation. icp_description says "B2B companies" while its funding language implies tech-only. Nothing anywhere says a lead's self-report is weaker than a detected fact. The model filled every gap — inventing "not B2B SaaS" as a disqualifier (#35), asserting headcounts absent from the data (#36), reading a scoring ideal as a hard gate. Config-as-data only beats prose-in-a-prompt if the data is actually specified; ours was half-specified and the unspecified half is where all three bugs live.
- The spot-check took three batches and still produced no usable weight data. Each failed differently: batch 1 had zero tier variance (competitor over-trigger), batch 2 was anchored (pipeline scores seen before judging), batch 3 was off-ICP and gate-capped on 7 of 12. Two of three were preventable by writing the protocol before running the batch instead of after. The protocol now exists in RUNBOOK, written from the failures.
- An accepted tradeoff silently changed severity, and a requirement vanished with its container. #23 documented "leads with no domain and no company alert on every submission" as acceptable when the cost was a spurious email; #29 wired scoring to that same anyOk branch and converted it into lead loss, unnoticed. Separately, Sprint 2's review recording lived on #25's acceptance criteria — #25 was superseded by #30 and the requirement disappeared with it, noticed only today, incidentally. Same shape as #37's board-Status gap: recorded in one place, silently dropped when the container moved.

One change next sprint:
- Before attaching a new consumer to an existing branch, gate, status field, or config field, re-read what it actually means and confirm its previously-accepted costs still hold. Structural per the never-a-reminder rule: a CLAUDE.md DoD line — "If this issue consumes an existing branch, gate, status field, or config value, name the decision that accepted its current behavior and confirm that acceptance still holds under the new consumer." #34 would have surfaced at #29's design stage rather than two sprints later, and #25's superseding would have flagged its orphaned recording AC.

## Sprint 4 Retro — 2026-08-03 (Jul 27–31; goal PARTIALLY met — the delivery half shipped and was live-verified on its named day, the alert half was never started; 1 committed issue closed (#40) + #38 deployed and live-verified but held open by its own close-together framing; #34/#39/#41/#42 untouched — so 2 of 6 did real shipped work, not 1; 3 new Backlog issues filed — #43/#44/#45; work stopped after Wed Jul 29, retro 3 days late)

What worked:
- Splitting an issue when the evidence said its scope was two problems, not one. #38 was chartered to close #35 and #36 together; grounding fixed #36 outright (message-wins precedence live-verified three ways — Ignite Visibility's own "700+" enrichment losing to its message's ~120, Aerotek/Grainger discarding via honest low scores instead of a world-knowledge size gate, a constructed IBM case holding against a genuine 300,000+ figure) while #35's competitor axis proved unreachable by prompt work. Holding #38 open until both closed would have blocked #40 and cost the sprint its one shipped deliverable. Instead the dependency was re-evaluated explicitly and written down (#38's real contribution to #40 was a trustworthy scorer, which was deployed; the #35 residual under-delivers wrongly-disqualified leads but cannot corrupt a HubSpot write), and #40 landed on its named day. Sprint 2's named-day practice is now 2 for 2 on goal issues.
- A prompt fix that failed twice got a measurement instead of a third rewrite. The name-scrub experiment on the Sanity fixture — identical enrichment and message, name scrubbed to a neutral placeholder, frozen snapshot behind a throwaway harness so scraping variance couldn't confound it — returned 60% disqualified with the real name (n=10) vs 16.7% scrubbed (n=6), a 3.6× effect, plus a check on the single treatment failure confirming no name leakage in its reasoning. That converted "the prompt still doesn't work" into a root cause no amount of wording could reach (a pre-textual name-recognition prior) and a specific fix (#44's config-maintained list, checked in code), while separately establishing a real ~15–20% description-driven tail that justifies keeping #43. The alternative — iterate the prompt until it looks fixed — is unbounded and would have produced no number to stop on.
- Sprint 3's "one change" fired on its first real occasion, unprompted. #40 attached new consumers to existing structures and named the decisions that accepted their behavior before doing so: the original `leads` migration's "HubSpot is write-only downstream" principle (which is why `hubspot_contact_id` is persisted at intake rather than read back at delivery), and #24's job-change asymmetry (which is why the column carries no uniqueness constraint). The same instinct produced the sprint's cleanest design call — delivery failures dead-letter without reverting `status` from `scored`, because a scoring failure means "redo the Claude call" and a delivery failure means "retry the write," and conflating them would misrepresent trustworthy data as needing a redo. Live-forced and confirmed, alert wording included.

What didn't:
- The sprint stopped on day 3 and nothing surfaced it. Jul 27 planned, Jul 28–29 worked, Jul 30–31 produced nothing, and nothing since — 1 of 6 committed issues closed, with #41 next, unblocked, and its one open design question already resolved and documented on Jul 29. No blocker is recorded because none existed. The stall is invisible in every artifact we keep: LOG.md has no Jul 30 or Jul 31 lines at all (absence, not a record of absence), the board still shows #35/#36/#38 as In Progress five days after anything progressed, and the milestone's only date was the goal issue's. Sprint 2's fix put a named day on the goal issue and it worked — but a single dated issue means everything sequenced after it can slip silently, which is precisely what the four untouched issues did. The deeper point is structural, not corrective: solo Scrum has no second person to notice a dropped thread. In a team a stalled sprint surfaces at someone else's standup; solo, the sprint is the person, so stepping away doesn't stall it — it stops it, silently, until they return. No board field or hook can make the sprint continue in an absence; the most any mechanism can do is make the stop visible on return. That's a property of the method chosen, worth naming honestly rather than pretending a process tweak closes it.
- Every-session steps keep failing, and the fix we already applied to them is the same class of thing that failed. Four instances in three working days: the LOG hook missed the 28th (2nd occurrence — the Sprint 2 retro flagged this exact failure), CLAUDE.md's "Current status" went stale in the same session, #37's board auto-add gap recurred a 3rd time with #43/#44/#45 landing statusless (corrected in-session), and #35 was silently auto-closed by a docs-only commit whose message quoted "closes #35" from the issue's own scope — caught only because the routine `gh project item-list` check ran. Sprint 1's one change was "process failures get structural fixes, never reminders." But the LOG hook *is* that fix, and it has now missed twice, because it is a prose instruction in CLAUDE.md executed by an agent that has to remember to read it — a reminder that changed address. The only rule from that sprint that has never failed is `check-env.sh`, which is a script that runs.
- The riskiest, least-bounded item was scheduled first and in the blocking position, and the scope cut made to fit the sprint bought room for an issue that never happened. #38 was a model-behavior fix: there is no way to know how many iterations it takes until you measure, which is exactly what the sprint had to discover mid-flight. It didn't close, it spawned three more Backlog issues (#43/#44/#45), and it left #35 and #36 open behind it. Meanwhile #34 (genuine lead loss) was pulled *into* Sprint 4 under the slip rule, error-alert formalization was pushed to Sprint 5 to make room for it, and #34 was then never picked up — the cut was paid for and the seat went empty. Related: the goal statement itself was two deliverables joined by "and" ("written to HubSpot **with** a hot-lead alert fired"), which is why this sprint cannot be honestly scored met or not-met on its own terms.

One change next sprint:
-  The generalized lesson, since this is the second sprint a "structural" fix in this class turned out to be a reminder: any instruction that must be remembered and read each session is a reminder wearing a mechanism's clothes — the LOG hook proved it by missing twice, while check-env.sh has never failed because it runs rather than gets read. So the standard tightens: an every-session ritual isn't fixed until it executes or renders on its own, without being read. First application this sprint — every committed issue gets a target day at planning, living on the board as a date field, not in prose. A `Target day` field on the GitHub Projects board is visible every time the board is opened, so a card sitting past its date in a non-Done column is a thing you see rather than a thing you check. Its boundary, named so Sprint 5 doesn't rediscover it: a board date surfaces a slip only when the board is opened, and during a multi-day absence the board isn't opened — so this catches slippage while working, not while away. The absence case (per "what didn't") has no mechanism fix and needs a different answer, likely a personal one about how solo work resumes after a gap. Applied to Sprint 4, the Jul 30 stall would have shown as three overdue cards on Jul 31 instead of surfacing at retro five days later. Corollary adopted at the same time: a sprint goal names one deliverable — if it contains "and," it is two goals and gets scored as two.

What didn't:
- The Target day board field — declared structural in the Sprint 4 one-change — was never implemented. Target days for Sprint 5 lived in milestone description prose, the exact medium the change was written to escape. This is the third consecutive sprint where a fix declared structural stayed prose. The test from Sprint 4: "isn't fixed until it executes or renders on its own, without being read." The board field doesn't exist; it never ran the test.

Same-company comparison: input tokens grew 1.13×, output tokens 1.68×; since output is priced 5× input, output drives it. Still under $0.02, but headroom went from ~25% to ~15% on worst observed. The generalizable miss: re-running a fixture is a free A/B on every metric it records. Sprint 6 inherits a hypothesis (output growth driven by v2 schema's per-dimension evidence + unconditional drafting) instead of a cold start.
- The DECISIONS entry for config bump 1.3.0-v1 was missed and only surfaced when a direct question was asked. Same class as the LOG hook: a prose convention that must be remembered to fire.
- Polish scope (README, architecture diagram, cost analysis, Loom, load test) had no issues. Closing milestone #5 didn't lose them — they were never issued in the first place. The milestone's open_issues count was 0 when it closed; the items existed only as prose in the milestone description. Non-issued scope is invisible to the board and to any milestone hygiene check.

One change next sprint:
- Before starting any issue, confirm every design decision in the body is ratified — a DECISIONS.md entry exists, or it's explicitly marked architect-approved. If the body reads "proposed," "needs ratification," or "TBD on design," stop and surface it before writing any code. This is a pickup-time lookup, not a mid-implementation judgment call. Structural because it fires on a discrete self-announcing event (picking up an issue) with a concrete lookup-shaped check — same trigger shape as the board DoD check and the commit-keyword rule, both of which have held every sprint. Corollary adopted at the same time: any scope item that will be done this sprint must exist as a GitHub issue before the sprint starts — prose in a milestone description is not a commitment.
